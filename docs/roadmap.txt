CCDN THEOREM 3: FORMAL PROOF ROADMAP
=================================

Target: N_aff(X,B) <= C(n) d^3 B^(1/d) (log B + d), uniformly in X,
for n >= 2, integral affine curves over Q of degree d >= 1, and real B >= 1.

The target remains unproved. Ordinary hypotheses on intermediate lemmas do not
discharge these obligations. No external counting result is accepted as an axiom.

Representation
--------------
Integer boxes use floor(B), with a proved equivalence to |x_i| <= B.
PlaneCurveBound uses irreducible rational bivariate polynomials and totalDegree.
AffineCurveBound uses prime ideals and eventual linear growth of the filtered
coordinate ring. HasAffineCurveDegree I d means dim(F_M(Q[x]/I)) = d*M+c for all
sufficiently large M. Each F_M is proved finite-dimensional and the slope is
proved unique. The standard geometric correspondence and the principal-plane
Hilbert-function formula still need Lean proofs.

Completed prerequisite: Bombieri--Vaaler (CCDN Theorem 3.3.4)
---------------------------------------------------------
ArithmeticHeights.BombieriVaaler contains the small integer kernel vector theorem,
including the gcd of maximal minors. Its 25-module dependency is vendored from
rwst/Subspace-Theorems at 09835cb23557ae5ae0cf55a9c4ab9055f5d60338.
It has been rebuilt on Lean/Mathlib 4.35.0. Axioms.lean audits its exported
declarations and their transitive axiom dependencies.

Completed assembly steps
------------------------
1. A nonzero integer coefficient vector on distinct monomials gives a nonzero
   polynomial, with the expected degree and coefficient bounds.
2. Evaluation-matrix kernel vectors give vanishing polynomials. Rational row
   span transports vanishing from selected rows to the larger point set.
3. Bombieri--Vaaler produces a small vanishing polynomial when the selected
   evaluation rows are independent and fewer than the available monomials.
4. A nonzero integer multiple has a leading coefficient at least as large in
   absolute value as the factor's. The explicit strict determinant threshold
   therefore implies nondivisibility. The threshold is NOT proved for curves.
5. The archimedean bound |det(A*A^T)| <= s! (N*H^2)^s and monomial bounds
   supply the upper-bound side of the determinant argument.
6. Cauchy--Binet yields determinant divisibility from weighted factorizations
   over any commutative ring. Repeated weights give an exponent at least
   s^2/(2*mu)-s. For univariate integer polynomial evaluations in one residue
   class the determinant and gcd of maximal minors are divisible by
   p^(s*(s-1)/2). Constructing the requisite factorization from a curve's local
   ring, and proving the geometric multiplicity bound, remain unfinished.
7. The real inequalities eliminating coefficient height, absorbing additive
   errors, specializing degrees 2..24, and absorbing logs are proved.
8. Distinct prime-power divisibilities combine into a product divisor and
   the associated logarithmic lower bound. The analytic prime-sum estimates
   and control of bad reduction have not been proved.

Next major milestone: global determinant lower bound (Proposition 3.2.6)
------------------------------------------------------------------------
A. Local algebra and multiplicity: Section 2, especially Lemma 2.4 and
   Corollary 2.5. Construct the local-ring filtrations and establish the
   p-adic determinant valuation from their graded dimensions.
B. Aggregate residue classes (Proposition 2.6). Prove the convexity estimate
   preserving the leading constant in the determinant exponent.
C. Bound point counts with multiplicity over finite fields (Lemma 2.7).
   For the plane-curve route only n=1 in the projective hypersurface argument
   is needed, but this still requires nontrivial finite-field geometry.
D. Bound bad primes using effective irreducibility/Noether forms (Section 3.2),
   including Ruppert's theorem as used in Theorem 3.2.2. Prove the required
   weighted prime-sum estimates rather than assuming them.
E. Combine the local divisibility exponents into a lower bound for the gcd
   of all maximal evaluation minors, retaining coefficient and degree control.

Then: projective auxiliary-polynomial degree (Theorem 3.1.1)
----------------------------------------------------------
Prove independent-row selection and the exact Hilbert-function count for
homogeneous polynomials modulo a principal ideal. Combine the lower bound
with the checked small-polynomial and nondivisibility steps. Establish the
coefficient-position reduction and controlled integer changes of coordinates.
This route must also handle irreducible but not absolutely irreducible inputs.

Then: affine plane counting (Proposition 4.2.1, Corollary 4.2.4)
-------------------------------------------------------------
Prove the primitive normalization, homogenization and scaling/prime selection
arguments, including the cases of a zero constant term and small box radius.
Prove the sharp intersection bound needed to turn an auxiliary polynomial
into a point count. Apply the already checked coefficient-height elimination.
Discharge PlaneCurveBound without unproved hypotheses.

Then: arbitrary ambient dimension (Proposition 4.3.1)
---------------------------------------------------
Construct controlled integral projections preserving degree and birationality,
bound their exceptional fibers, and formalize the non-geometrically-integral
case. Relate the geometric degree to the target's filtered-coordinate-ring
definition. Track the constants so they depend only on ambient dimension.
Discharge AffineCurveBound without unproved hypotheses.

Pierce integration
------------------
Only after the full input required by the application has an unconditional
proof and passes its axiom audit should pierce-length-bound acquire a pinned
dependency on this repository. This repository does not resolve the separate
Pierce resultant, geometry, or exceptional-pattern counting obligations.
