Factor convention
-----------------
The literal Definition 3.2.1 excludes primes where the reduced polynomial
fails absolute irreducibility. HypersurfaceBadReduction additionally excludes
primes where the reduction loses degree. For nonzero homogeneous equations
these agree. They need not agree for affine equations.

Our independently checked example is f(x,y)=439*x^2+y, with homogeneous
equation F(X,Y,Z)=439*X^2+Y*Z. The degree is two, the cutoff is 432, and 439
is prime. Every affine reduction is absolutely irreducible: its equation
solves uniquely for y over every field. Thus the literal affine factor is 1.
At 439 the homogeneous reduction is Y*Z, whereas all other reductions are
irreducible. Its factor is exp(log(439)/439)>1. The existing inclusive factor
hypersurfaceBadPrimeFactor has the latter value because it includes the
degree drop; the new literalAffineBadPrimeFactor has value 1.

This refutes the intermediate equality between the literal affine and
homogeneous factors asserted in the proof of Proposition 4.2.1. It does NOT
refute the proposition's auxiliary-polynomial conclusion. The checked author
addenda do not address this distinction. A closure-based interpretation of
the affine factor is a possible convention, not a confirmed author intention.

The formal theorem affine_hypersurface_auxiliary uses the repository's
degree-loss-inclusive factor throughout. It proves all box and coefficient
cases, including the minimum bound and bounded translations. The new
literal_affine_hypersurface_auxiliary proves the literal printed version by
the chart argument below. A uniform constant comparing the two factors
would not be a valid repair: replacing 439 by products of arbitrarily
many large primes gives literal factor 1 and an unbounded inclusive factor.
The coefficient-uniform consequences may be proved directly from the existing
variant and its height estimate.

In degree at least two, the inclusive affine factor equals its homogeneous
factor. In degree one only the proved inequality is used: p*x+1 can become
constant affinely but remains an irreducible homogeneous linear equation.

Coefficient dichotomy
---------------------
Lemma 4.2.2 is now proved with the printed dimension-only multiplier by
projective_coefficient_height_dichotomy_uniform. In n+2 variables, one
C(n)>0 works for every nonzero homogeneous degree-d integer polynomial
and every B>=1:
H(f)<=C(n)*B^(d*N), N=choose(n+1+d,d), or an actual homogeneous degree-d
nonmultiple vanishes on all projective zeros of height at most B.
Primitivity and irreducibility are not needed. Primitive evaluation rows
are embedded into the hyperplane X0=0; its proved determinant divisor
absorbs the factorial interpolation cost. This argument does not use the
disputed affine-factor equality. The earlier N^N multiplier remains a
valid quantitative bound, sufficient for the polynomial box cutoff.
The literal affine-factor conclusions of 4.2.1 and 4.2.4 are now proved by
the chart argument and final assembly below.

Exact chart determinant and literal-factor branches
--------------------------------------------------
LiteralAffineBadPrimes now defines the literal factor, including value zero
for a nonabsolute equation, and proves finiteness and translation invariance.
ProjectiveOpenResidueDeterminant proves the sharp prime-power divisor on
the open set where the extra reduced factor is nonzero. Applied in
AffineChartPrimeDeterminant, a degree-dropping reduction is written as
g*X0^(d-e), where g is the homogenization of the actual degree-e affine
reduction. At every supplied point the first coordinate is a p-adic unit;
the extra X0 factor therefore contributes no local multiplicity. The sharp
divisor with the original degree d follows from the proved counts for g.

AffineChartGlobalDeterminant and the chart auxiliary modules propagate this
through the prime sum, rank comparison, height ranges and coefficient
positioning. LiteralScaledAuxiliary constructs the actual finite point
family and excluded primes. LiteralAffineMinimum proves the full printed
minimum with literalAffineBadPrimeFactor in two cases: an actual admissible
scaling prime, and B<=S*(d+1)^k with fixed S>=1 and k. Both construct proper
auxiliary polynomials, under primitivity and absolute irreducibility.
There is no supplied finite-field count or local determinant premise.

LiteralAffineAuxiliaryTheorem completes all radii, the zero-constant case
by bounded integral translation, and the nonabsolute case. Its endpoint
literal_affine_hypersurface_auxiliary has only the printed primitivity,
irreducibility, positive degree and B>=1 assumptions. No geometric
irreducibility or chosen-prime hypothesis remains. The dimension-only
constant and original leading coefficient height are retained.

LiteralAffinePlaneCount proves literal_affine_plane_coefficient_count_uniform
for the actual affine integer zero locus, with one absolute positive constant
chosen before the equation and B. Rational nondivisibility and Bezout are
discharged internally. These prove the literal conclusions of Proposition
4.2.1 and Corollary 4.2.4 while retaining the counterexample to the printed
intermediate equality.

Published corrections affecting future work
------------------------------------------
The author's addenda correct the variable count in Proposition 2.6 to n+2,
strengthen the leading-form assumption in 4.3.3 to absolute irreducibility,
replace d^14 by d^18 in 4.3.4, and add a factor d to the degree in 4.3.7.
Remark 4.3.8 must use the corrected statements. These targets are recorded in
full-paper-plan.txt. Our existing residue-determinant indexing already has
the corrected variable count.
