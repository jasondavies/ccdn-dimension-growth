1. Arbitrary weighted profiles (Lemma2.2)

WeightedSequenceEstimate.lean proves the following for every dimension m>=2.
Let g:N->N, g(0)=1, mu>0, c in N, and
  g(k) <= mu*k^(m-1)/(m-1)! + c*mu*k^(m-2)   (k>0).
For every finite list of s weights in which weight k occurs at most g(k)
times:
  sum(weights) >= (m!/mu)^(1/m)*m/(m+1)*s^(1+1/m)
                   - (m+1+c*(m-1)!)*s.
There is no restriction that g be a hypersurface Hilbert function.
There is no assumed cumulative estimate: the shifted binomial capacity
is derived internally from the pointwise hypothesis. The explicit error
is independent of mu. c=0 is allowed.

The initial-segment endpoint assumes an infinite enumeration in which the
set of indices of each weight has exactly g(k) members, with g(k)>0.
It proves the paper's sum over the first s terms. Monotonicity is not needed:
an arbitrary order can only make this lower-bound problem harder, and the
finite-list proof already treats every order.

Dimension1 requires care in the literal arbitrary-profile formulation.
With k^(m-2) interpreted as 1/k, the printed m=1 uniform claim is false.
WeightedDimensionOneExample.lean now formalizes the counterexample:
no_uniform_dimension_one_profile_error constructs, for every real C, an
actual positive profile and infinite monotone enumeration with exact finite
fibers, and an initial segment violating s^2/(2mu)-C*s. Its construction is:
  c=1, mu=K!, g(0)=1, g(k)=mu+floor(mu/k) for k>0.
Writing H_K=sum_{k=1}^K 1/k, the nondecreasing enumeration through weight K
has exactly
  s=1+mu*(K+H_K), E=sum(weights)=mu*K*(K+1)/2+mu*K.
Since k divides mu for 1<=k<=K, these are exact integer equalities.
For H_K>=3/2,
  [s^2/(2mu)-E]/s >= (H_K-3/2)/3.
Indeed s<=3muK and s^2/(2mu)-E>=muK*(H_K-3/2).
The right side is unbounded with K, contradicting any error C_{1,1}*s.
Thus the m=1 boundary cannot be silently included with the printed
arbitrary-g assumption and mu-independent error.

This does not affect the actual dimension1 hypersurface application:
its layers obey g(k)<=mu, a stronger condition. The checked theorem
weighted_sequence_dimension_one proves the sharp leading term with
error 2s for every list with at most mu occurrences of each weight.
The existing hypersurface determinant pipeline already has this valid
dimension1 case.

2. Unequal homogeneous column degrees (Corollary2.5)

The rendered Corollary2.5 explicitly uses primitive integer tuples and
homogeneous columns, but states no equality of column degrees and no
replacement of evaluations by normalized chart functions.

For every s and prime p>s, take the SAME fixed smooth line X2=0, points
  z_i=(i+1,p,0), 0<=i<s,
and columns
  F_j=X0^j, 0<=j<s.
MixedDegreeProjectiveExample.lean checks:
* z_i is primitive;
* z_i lies on X2=0;
* every point has exactly the same actual projective reduction [1:0:0];
* F_j is homogeneous of degree j;
* the evaluation determinant is a Vandermonde determinant prime to p.
It additionally proves no_uniform_mixed_degree_local_error:
for EVERY real C, one can choose s,p so that no exponent e satisfying
  s^2/2-C*s <= e
has p^e dividing the unnormalized determinant.
This is a family, not a single small numerical example hidden by an
unspecified asymptotic error. The error has precisely the printed n=1,
mu=1 shape.

The flaw in treating the displayed primitive evaluations as local ring
functions is that passage to the chart X0!=0 changes F_j(z_i) to
  F_j(z_i)/z_i0^(deg F_j).
For common column degree this is one invertible factor per matrix row.
For different degrees it is an entry-dependent factor and cannot be
discarded by determinant row scaling.

MixedDegreeProjectiveLocal.lean proves the corrected local assertion:
if N is the actual proved hypersurface multiplicity exponent and u_i
is an inverse of the pivot coordinate modulo p^N, then
  p^N | det(u_i^(deg F_j) * F_j(z_i)).
The existence theorem constructs those inverse coordinates from the
chart-unit hypothesis. It assumes no new count or factorization statement.

The already checked common-degree Corollary2.5/2.9 and Proposition3.2.6
pipeline remains valid and unchanged. The normalized mixed-degree theorem
does NOT by itself prove a simultaneous-prime gcd inequality for a single
unnormalized integer matrix: the normalizations can depend on the prime.
The local example refutes the literal primitive-evaluation local statement.
It does not by itself refute the global proposition. A separate global
construction now does so, as recorded in section 6 below.

4. General Noetherian-local determinant lemma

PrimeIntegerLocalDeterminant proves exact Lemma 2.4 with the actual target
Z_(p), constructed as Localization.AtPrime(span{p:Z}). Its residue map to
ZMod p and kernel span{p} are proved from localization.

The source is an arbitrary commutative Noetherian local ring R. The actual
evaluation maps R -> Z_(p) supply the residue-field identification. No
chosen inclusion Z_(p) -> R is needed. localPrimeLayerDimension measures the
image of the kth maximal-ideal power in A/m_A^(k+1), where A=R/(p).
IdealAdicLayerQuotient identifies that image with the literal quotient
m_A^k/m_A^(k+1); the residue-field equivalence identifies its scalar field.

primeInteger_local_enumerated_weight_det gives the exact exponent from a
monotone enumeration with the actual layer multiplicities. The finite-layer
generators and lifting factorizations are constructed from Noetherianity.
ExactFiltrationWeights constructs finite initial segments and proves their
minimal-weight property, including profiles with zero layers.

primeInteger_local_det_profile_dichotomy handles finite profiles without an
extra capacity assumption: it constructs the first s weights and their
determinant divisor, or proves the determinant zero by Krull intersection
when the profile has fewer than s weights.

This closes the full local-ring scope of Lemma 2.4. The later local Hilbert
and weight constructions below also give the exact 2.1 equality and 2.3
interface. The mixed-degree normalization issue in Corollary 2.5 remains.

5. Actual hypersurface stalks and their weight sequences

HypersurfacePointStalk constructs the localization of K[x]/(f) at the
maximal ideal of a K-rational zero a of f. Its residue field is identified
with K. InitialFormMultiplication and HypersurfaceTangentEquality prove
cancellation and the entire initial-form kernel, giving the exact layer
formula determined by the first nonzero translated homogeneous component.
Quotient, localization and translation equivalences transport this equality
to the actual stalk. hypersurfacePointStalk_hilbert_eq applies over every
field, with no supplied tangent-cone equality or Hilbert estimate.

ActualLocalHilbertWeights.exists_hypersurfacePointStalk_hilbert_weights
constructs the multiplicity mu, exact profile and infinite monotone weight
enumeration directly from f!=0 and f(a)=0. For positive hypersurface
dimension m it proves
  sum(first s weights) >= (m!/mu)^(1/m)*m/(m+1)*s^(1+1/m) - (m+1)*s.
InfiniteWeightEnumeration constructs the exact finite fibers. The sharp
bound uses the cumulative binomial profile, including dimension one, and
does not use the false arbitrary-profile extension above.

This proves the affine-chart formulation of the local Hilbert equality and
Lemma 2.3. A separate Scheme/Proj stalk comparison is not claimed.

HypersurfacePointwiseRemainder now proves the separate pointwise estimate
printed in Lemma 2.1. For each positive dimension m there exists C>=0, chosen
before every field K, equation f!=0 and rational zero a, such that the
constructed actual multiplicity mu and localized layer dimensions satisfy
  g(k) <= mu*k^(m-1)/(m-1)! + C*k^((m:Real)-2), k>0.
For m=1 the error is zero, including the literal negative exponent.
PolynomialUniformDifference proves the required uniform polynomial difference
estimate, independent of the step mu. No Hilbert or analytic estimate remains
as a supplied premise.


6. Global differing-degree Proposition 3.2.6

GridVandermondeCounterexample and GlobalMixedDegreeDeterminant prove a
separate global counterexample. The printed statement allows individually
homogeneous forms without a common-degree condition, and includes d=1.
Set n=d=L=1 and f=X2. The line equation is primitive and absolutely
irreducible, and its defined bad-prime factor is exactly one.

For N>=2 and Q=N!, use the N^2 primitive tuples
  (i*Q+1,j+1,0), 0<=i,j<N,
and the monomials X0^a*X1^b, 0<=a,b<N, of degrees a+b. Primitivity follows
from j+1 dividing Q. The evaluation matrix is a Kronecker product of two
Vandermonde matrices. Its determinant D_N is positive and satisfies
  D_N <= (N*N!)^(N*choose(N,2))*N^(N*choose(N,2)),
  log|D_N| <= (3/4)*N^4*log(N).
The printed lower bound would require
  log|D_N| >= (N^4/2)*(log(N^2)-C)
for a uniform real C. Choosing log(N)>2C contradicts that inequality.

no_uniform_global_mixed_degree_det_lower_bound negates the universal
single-line specialization with primitive tuples and homogeneous forms
indexed by Fin(s), in the printed orientation (F_i(xi_j)). All concrete
hypotheses and nonvanishing are proved. The absolute gcd of a singleton
is the absolute value of its determinant, so L=1 suffices. The sharper
superfactorial identity is unnecessary and is not claimed as an endpoint.

This refutes the unrestricted differing-degree formulation of the GLOBAL
proposition. The common-degree determinant theorem used by the auxiliary
polynomial and main counting results remains valid.
