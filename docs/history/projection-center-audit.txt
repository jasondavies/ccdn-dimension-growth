Projection centers: complete statements and source corrections
================================================================

Source: CCDN, The dimension growth conjecture, polynomial in the degree
and without logarithmic factors, Section 5, pp. 2287--2290.
DOI: https://doi.org/10.2140/ant.2020.14.2261

1. Actual complementary projection and height
---------------------------------------------

BoundedCenterProjection, CenterProjectionHeight and CenterProjectionFormula
start from actual independent integer center rows A_i, with r rows and
|A_ij|<=T. They construct a nonzero integer D and an integer matrix P with
P^2=D*P and entries bounded by 2*r!*T^r. The rational map Q=D^(-1)*P is an
idempotent with kernel exactly span(A_i), complementary image, and formula
Qx=x-sum_i L_i(x)*A_i, where L_i(A_j)=delta_ij. Its intrinsic projective
height satisfies H(Qx)<=2*(n+1)*r!*T^r*H(x) outside the center.

For T=(m+1)^2*d^2 and r=n-m-1>=1, the constructed complementary space has
vector dimension m+2 and the proved bound implies exactly
H(Qx)<=C(n)*d^(2*r^2)*H(x), as in (5-5). The constant is chosen before
the degree and center. Actual integral representatives come from P applied
to the primitive input. If every center row has coordinate j equal to zero,
Q preserves that coordinate and (Pz)_j=D*z_j. This gives the fixed nonzero
denominator needed for the affine application.

The construction uses a common nonzero minor and its cofactors. It does
not use Lemma 5.4's incorrect square-root constant. This construction from
bounded center rows is distinct from constructing a geometrically good center.

GeometricProjectionCenter now constructs such a center as the kernel of an
actual integer matrix. Its vector dimension is n-m-1, it is disjoint from
the variety over extension fields, and its projection is finite and
birational with geometrically integral image of exactly degree d.
IndependentBirationalProjection ensures full row rank even in degree one:
if the primitive row is redundant, the normalization already generates
the field and a standard unit row replaces it. The original integer
projection-matrix bound is preserved. SharpBoundedBirationalCenter now
constructs a good center with actual independent integer basis rows bounded
by T=(m+1)^2*d^2. SharpGoodCenterProjection combines those rows with the
complementary projection construction: the image is geometrically integral
of unchanged dimension and degree, and the height bound on X has exactly
the printed exponent 2*r^2. The constant depends only on the ambient
dimension. SharpAmbientCenterProjection gives the full ambient estimate
for every rational point outside the constructed center, together with
explicit geometric disjointness and the actual complementary image. The fixed-coordinate endpoint places
the center in X0=0 for an affine closure and includes the empty center.
ZeroDimensionalGoodCenter proves that a geometrically integral
zero-dimensional source has degree one and constructs zero-one center rows;
this handles the dimension-zero boundary without a false strict degree bound.

IntegralProjectionSpecialization supplies monic coordinate equations on
every geometric fiber. HomogeneousProjectionNoBasePoint uses them to rule
out nonzero points in the projection kernel over every infinite extension
field. IntegralProjectionImage also proves that an integral polynomial map
over an algebraically closed field has image exactly the zero locus of its
kernel, using lying over and the Nullstellensatz.

ZeroColumnMinorSpecialization strengthens the proved polynomial
specialization step of Lemma 5.3 to preserve any specified zero columns.
From a homogeneous maximal-minor polynomial and an actual independent
rational witness obeying those constraints, it constructs integer rows
with entries at most max(1,deg F), full rank and preserved nonvanishing.
Every maximal minor has magnitude at most r!*max(1,deg F)^r. The
degree-zero case is included. SharpBirationalCenterNonvanishing constructs
the required geometric witness within X0=0, and SharpBoundedBirationalCenter
applies this specialization to construct the bounded center there.

2. Literal Lemma 5.2 at dimension zero and degree one
----------------------------------------------------

The printed hypotheses allow m=0,d=1,n=2. OriginProjectivePoint constructs
the actual projective ideal of [1:0:0]: it is homogeneous, geometrically
prime, has cone Hilbert dimension one and multiplicity one, and its locus
is exactly that point. A center in G(0,2) is itself a point of P^2. Its
actual one-row maximal minors are its projective coordinates.

Every nonempty locus G of centers disjoint from this X omits [1:0:0], so
its complement is nonempty and proper. The asserted strict degree bound
is (m+1)^2*d^2=1. Every equation of degree below one is constant; even an
arbitrary infinite family of such equations has either empty or full zero
locus. Thus such a family cannot cut out the complement of G.

ZeroDimensionalCenterObstruction proves this directly, including an endpoint
in actual maximal-minor coordinates. It quantifies every nonempty G obeying
the necessary disjointness condition; it does not define or construct the
birational good-center locus itself. This already obstructs the printed
conjunction of nonemptiness, disjointness and the strict defining-degree
bound. No positive-dimensional counterexample is asserted.

3. Chow incidence and sharp center detection
---------------------------------------------

ChowIncidenceIdeal constructs the actual elimination kernel for hyperplane
tuples meeting an integral affine variety (a prime coordinate ideal) on
x0=1 and proves it prime. Over an
algebraically closed field, ChowIncidenceLocus identifies it exactly with
the vanishing ideal of the actual incidence set. ChowIncidenceGrading
proves separate homogeneity in the hyperplane coefficient blocks.
ChowIncidenceNonzero proves the kernel nonzero when the number of
hyperplanes exceeds the actual filtered Hilbert dimension. Consequently,
ChowIncidenceEquation constructs an actual irreducible equation vanishing
on the incidence set. None of these conclusions assumes a supplied Chow
form.

ChowIncidencePrincipal now proves principality for m+1 hyperplanes on a
prime affine ideal of dimension m in positive ambient dimension. Generic
linear independence is constructed by deformation from an actual
algebraically independent coordinate tuple; a proper-section Hilbert argument proves that the kernel
is principal. PrincipalWeightedHomogeneous proves the generator homogeneous
separately in every hyperplane block. ChowIncidenceBaseChange proves exact
commutation with arbitrary field extension, for every source ideal.
ChowIncidenceForm constructs an absolutely irreducible generator when the
source is geometrically integral. No supplied Chow equation, genericity
or principality assumption remains in these results.

ProjectiveChowIncidence identifies this ideal with the vanishing ideal of
the full projective incidence locus on the actual closure. Polynomial pivot
parametrization and vanishing on a nonempty open part of a prime variety
cover the points at infinity. ProjectiveIncidenceClosed proves closedness
of the actual projective incidence image for every homogeneous ideal
over an algebraically closed field.
An empty fiber gives a positive power of the irrelevant ideal contained
in the incidence relations. Their degree-component coefficient matrix
then supplies a determinant which separates that fiber from the incidence
locus. ProjectiveChowEquation combines closedness with the constructed
generator: its vanishing is equivalent to actual projective intersection,
also after algebraically closed coefficient extension. ChowExactDegree and
ProjectiveChowExactDegree now prove the exact multidegree (d,...,d) in
characteristic zero, including equations over the original field for
geometrically integral sources and the zero-dimensional case.

GenericHyperplaneNormalization supplies actual integral normalization and
independent coordinates for algebraically independent hyperplane coefficients
over an algebraically closed extension where the source stays prime.
GenericHyperplaneAvoidance proves that these hyperplanes have no common
projective point. BalancedParameterFiniteness constructs finite module
generators from the resulting coordinate-power relations. The source is not
assumed finite over the parameters. ChowIncidenceHeight also proves that the
constructed principal incidence ideal has height exactly one. A named
universal coefficient-field construction is now supplied by
UniversalHyperplaneNormalization: rational coefficient variables give an
actual finite injective normalization of exact homogeneous field rank d.
GeometricIdealPrimality proves the needed primality over arbitrary field
extensions from primality over an algebraic closure.

HomogeneousNormalizationRank proves exact fraction-field rank d over the
actual parameter subfield of a homogeneous prime finite linear normalization
in characteristic zero. It constructs a primitive linear coordinate and its
homogeneous image equation, proves the equation monic up to a unit, and
identifies its degree with the field degree. AffineNormalizationRank now
transfers this exact rank to the actual affine function field for a finite
normalization fixing X0, including dimension zero. The cone is embedded in
the fraction field of the affine coordinate ring with a radial variable;
adjoining that variable preserves the parameter-field rank.

ClosureNormalizationInfinite constructs a finite normalization fixing X0
over every infinite field, including dimension zero. GenericLinearNormalizationOpenness
proves that one finite specialization of a polynomial family of homogeneous
linear maps makes its generic map finite. The proof constructs full-degree
generation certificates and a determinant that is nonzero at that specialization.
FixedCoordinateGenericNormalization applies this to the X0-fixed family
and proves injectivity of the affine parameters and homogeneous coordinates.

ChowRowSymmetry proves that all hyperplane block degrees of the constructed
generator agree. ChowTotalDegree gives total degree (m+1) times the common
block degree. ProjectiveChowWithoutChart and GeometricProjectiveChow remove
the prescribed standard-chart hypothesis, including for the equation over
the original coefficient field. ChowRowCoordinateDegree identifies a row
degree with a coordinate degree using a nonzero evaluation on its axis.
ChowIncidenceMinimalPolynomial identifies the distinguished coordinate
degree with the actual incidence minimal-polynomial degree.
GenericLinearPrimitive constructs a generic primitive linear coordinate in
characteristic zero from coordinates separating the embeddings of a finite
field extension, after adjoining rational parameters.
ScalarQuotientFractionField, PolynomialCoefficientNormalizationRank and
ChowNestedIncidence compare the actual quotient, fraction and parameter-field
models. ChowAxisEvaluation constructs the required nonzero axis evaluation.
ChowNormalizationDegreeComparison identifies the distinguished coefficient
degree with the affine normalization rank. ChowExactDegree concludes that
every block degree is d and the total degree is (m+1)*d.
ProjectiveChowChartReduction and ProjectiveChowExactDegree extend this to
arbitrary homogeneous prime sources with nonempty projective locus, without
a prescribed chart, and to geometrically integral sources over the original
characteristic-zero field. No supplied rank or degree-identification premise
remains in these exact-degree endpoints.

ChowPolynomialPower proves a separate algebraic step in (5-3): nonzero
polynomials with the same geometric zero locus, one irreducible, differ
by a nonzero scalar times a positive power of that irreducible polynomial.
It retains the scalar omitted by the printed identity and proves the
irreducibility criterion by equality of degrees.

ChowLinearSpecialization proves finiteness of a linear projection from a
disjoint center, lifts actual nonzero image-cone points, and identifies
projective incidence on the image. ChowProjectionCriterion constructs the
source and image equations without prescribed charts and proves the actual
restriction is c*G^k, with c nonzero and k positive. Nonzero restriction is
equivalent to center disjointness; it preserves the source total degree.

CenterMinorProjection and CenterMinorMatrix prove these facts for the
literal determinant projection: its common zero space is exactly the span
of the independent center rows. UniversalCenterChowRestriction constructs
the polynomial family in the actual Plucker coordinates. Every coefficient
is homogeneous of the source Chow total degree. CenterChowCriterion chooses
this family before the center and gives the nonvanishing and power criteria
for every independent center basis. The source total degree is now proved
to be (m+1)*d. CenterNoetherForms and GeometricCenterNoetherForms construct
a finite polynomial detector over the original characteristic-zero field.
Its polynomials are homogeneous of degree D*max(1,D^2-1), where D=(m+1)*d;
nonvanishing detects geometric irreducibility of the actual restriction.
The degree-one case is included. This earlier detector has a cubic degree
bound; the quadratic resultant construction below supplies the sharper
positive-dimensional estimate.

ProjectionImageFractionField constructs the actual image function field
inside the source field. HomogeneousProjectionRankBound proves
e*[F:L] <= d for a finite homogeneous linear projection, where d and e are
the actual source and image Hilbert multiplicities. HomogeneousProjectionBirational
therefore proves that these multiplicities are equal if and only if the
projection is birational in characteristic zero. It also proves the boundary
case of a zero-dimensional homogeneous cone. ChowRestrictionBirational now
combines this with the exact Chow degree: for a geometrically disjoint
center, the actual restriction is geometrically irreducible if and only
if the base-field projection is birational. BirationalCenterDetector
constructs a finite family over the original characteristic-zero field
whose nonvanishing is exactly disjointness and birationality, with degree
D*max(1,D^2-1). No Chow equation or image multiplicity is supplied.

BirationalMinorCenterWitness constructs actual independent rational center
rows whose literal determinant projection is integral and birational. Its
fixed-coordinate version puts the center in X0=0 for an affine closure,
including dimension zero. MatrixProjectionFields proves that the image
function field depends only on the matrix kernel, allowing the constructed
normalization and primitive coordinate to be replaced by the determinant
projection. These are actual witnesses, without a supplied good-center
existence hypothesis.

BirationalCenterNonvanishing selects one member of the detector family
nonzero at a constructed witness. In the projective case the base field is
Q; the fixed-coordinate affine version works over every characteristic-zero
field and includes dimension zero. It proves that nonvanishing of the
selected member at any independent center implies geometric disjointness
and birationality. The affine witness has zero first column, so the detector
also remains nonzero on that constrained parameter subspace.

AffinePointZariski defines the actual Zariski topology on field-valued affine
points via evaluation prime ideals. MatrixGoodCenterDensity proves that
nonzero polynomial conditions are dense and open in the full-row-rank matrix
space. ZeroColumnCenterDensity proves the constrained counterpart when the
polynomial restriction to the prescribed-zero-column subspace is nonzero;
an actual rational witness supplies this premise. Density in that subspace
is proved directly, rather than inferred from ambient density.
BirationalCenterDensity and FixedCoordinateBirationalCenterDensity now
apply these facts to the constructed actual birational-center locus.

CenterPluckerCoordinates constructs the injective Plucker map of actual
dimension-r subspaces, identifies equality with equality of row spans, and
relates homogeneous equations to actual maximal minors. Its coordinates
are indexed by all ordered column injections, so they contain signed
redundancies. CenterPluckerZariski uses the topology induced by homogeneous
principal opens in this projective space. BirationalCenterPluckerTopology
and FixedCoordinatePluckerTopology prove that the actual good-center locus
is dense and open in this topology, including the subspace of centers in
X0=0. No equality with the earlier quotient topology, binomial target
dimension, or Scheme/Proj identification is asserted.

The source says each coefficient of R_X,Lambda has Plucker degree
D=(m+1)*d. The cited universal Noether forms have degree D^2-1 in those
coefficients. Direct composition therefore gives degree at most
D*(D^2-1), not the claimed strict bound below D^2. This remains a gap in
the printed calculation, but a separate resultant argument is now proved.
GenericBlockLine and GenericBlockLineIrreducible replace one hyperplane
row by u+t*v and prove its exact degree and generic-line irreducibility.
BlockResultantIrreducibility uses the actual Chow power R=c*G^e: the
fixed-size derivative resultant is nonzero exactly when e=1.
ResultantCoefficientFamily and GenericBlockLineHomogeneity prove that its
parameter coefficients are homogeneous of degree D*(2*d-1) in the Plucker
variables. SharpCenterResultantDetector constructs this finite family over
the original characteristic-zero field and proves that it detects every
geometrically disjoint birational center over an algebraically closed
extension. SharpBirationalCenterDetector gives the base-field criterion.
For m>=1 the degree is strictly below D^2, as proved in SharpCenterBounds.
For m=0 geometric integrality forces d=1; degree-one equations and zero-one
center rows provide the valid replacement at the obstructed boundary.

4. General affine projection
----------------------------

The full general affine Proposition 4.3.1 is now proved by
exists_geometrically_integral_affine_projection and its uniform-constant
wrapper affine_variety_projection_constants. Actual integer finite
birational projections preserve the source degree, geometric integrality
and the exact kernel equation. The point-count factor is d and the box
loss is C(n)*d^(2n), with constants chosen before the ideal, degree and
radius. Every source dimension is covered, including dimension zero and
ambient dimension zero; the zero-dimensional case constructs its rational
point and proves degree one. The existing affine curve theorem supplies
the sharper additive-error clause.

ClosureProjectionBirational proves the needed closure field generation
from actual affine reconstruction identities. ProjectiveClosureHilbertPolynomial
and AffineProjectionDegree then prove exact degree. ProjectiveClosureRecovery
also proves that a homogeneous prime ideal with X0 outside it is recovered
as the closure of its standard chart, with the exact chart Hilbert dimension
and multiplicity.

5. Complete subspace statements
--------------------------------

SharpPluckerComplementEquations constructs source-field homogeneous equations
whose common zero set is exactly the complement of the actual good
CenterSubspace locus. The same theorem proves density, openness and the
strict quadratic degree bound in positive dimension, over any given field
extension of Q. SharpFixedPluckerComplementEquations gives the constrained
X0=0 result over any extension of a characteristic-zero source field.
Extension primality and actual good-center witnesses are proved internally.
The good-center condition retains disjointness over the algebraic closure
of the extension and actual function-field birationality.

BirationalCenterPluckerHeight constructs a rational CenterSubspace in the
actual good locus and proves its intrinsic height at most
r!*((m+1)^2*d^2)^r. The fixed-coordinate theorem also includes the empty
center. IntegerMinorProjectiveHeight supplies the height bound for bounded
integer maximal minors, and the proved basis-invariant Plucker map transfers
it to the actual center. No primitivity, good-center or rank premise is
supplied to the final existence theorem.

The literal boundary obstruction in Lemma 5.2 and the corrected constant
in Lemma 5.4 remain part of the formalisation. The positive-dimensional
quadratic degree bound uses the proved resultant argument; it does not
validate the printed Noether-composition degree calculation.
